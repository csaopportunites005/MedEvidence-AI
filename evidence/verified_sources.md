## 1. European Association of Urology – Guidelines on Prostate Cancer

**Organization:** European Association of Urology (EAU)

**Guideline:** EAU Guidelines on Prostate Cancer – Diagnostic Evaluation

**Version:** 2026

**Official source:**  
https://uroweb.org/guidelines/prostatecancer/chapter/diagnostic-evaluation

### Key points verified

The guideline states that:

- PSA is prostate-specific but not cancer-specific and may also be elevated in benign conditions such as benign prostatic hyperplasia and prostatitis.
- An abnormal digital rectal examination is an indication for prostate MRI, or direct biopsy when there is suspicion of extracapsular disease.
- Prostate MRI is initiated when prostate cancer is suspected based on PSA and/or digital rectal examination.
- MRI can help guide targeted prostate biopsy and provide local staging information.
- MRI should be performed before prostate biopsy in patients with suspected organ-confined disease.
- Prostate cancer is usually confirmed histopathologically using prostate biopsy.
- When MRI shows a suspicious lesion, MRI-targeted biopsy can be performed.
- A negative MRI does not automatically exclude biopsy when clinical suspicion remains high.
- PSMA PET/CT has an important role in staging, particularly in higher-risk disease.

### Relevance to the test case

The patient's PSA of 90 ng/mL and abnormal digital rectal examination represent findings that warrant a structured diagnostic assessment.

However:

**PSA elevation + abnormal DRE ≠ histological confirmation of prostate cancer.**

The diagnostic pathway should therefore distinguish:

**Clinical suspicion → Imaging / diagnostic assessment → Biopsy → Histopathological confirmation → Staging**

## 3. Verification Principle

MedEvidence-AI follows a simple rule:

> **A reference should not be presented as verified evidence unless the source can be independently checked.**

When a reference cannot be verified, the workflow should explicitly state:

**"Source not verified — do not use as definitive evidence."**

---

## 4. Limitations

Verification of a source does not automatically mean that every AI-generated statement is supported by that source.

Each important clinical claim must still be compared with the actual content of the cited guideline or publication.

Guidelines may also be updated over time. Therefore, the version and publication/update date should always be recorded.
