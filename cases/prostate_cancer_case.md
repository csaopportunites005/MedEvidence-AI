# Clinical Test Case – Suspected Prostate Cancer

## Case Summary

A 70-year-old man presents with:

- Dysuria (burning during urination)
- Pollakiuria (increased urinary frequency)
- Enlarged prostate on digital rectal examination
- Painless prostate
- Presence of prostatic nodules
- Total PSA: 90 ng/mL
- Median sulcus: not specified

## Clinical Question

How should the diagnostic workup be structured in a patient with clinical and biological findings suspicious for prostate cancer?

## Objectives

The purpose of this case is to evaluate whether an AI-assisted clinical research workflow can:

1. Structure the clinical information.
2. Identify the main diagnostic hypothesis and relevant differential diagnoses.
3. Identify missing clinical information.
4. Propose a structured diagnostic pathway.
5. Distinguish clinical suspicion from histological confirmation.
6. Identify appropriate evidence and clinical guidelines.
7. Verify the sources supporting important clinical recommendations.
8. Explicitly report uncertainty when evidence is incomplete.

## Important Clinical Principle

A high PSA level and an abnormal digital rectal examination can strongly increase suspicion for prostate cancer, but they do not by themselves constitute histological confirmation.

The workflow should therefore distinguish between:

**Clinical suspicion → Diagnostic investigation → Histopathological confirmation → Staging**

## Missing Information

The following information is not provided and may be relevant to the diagnostic assessment:

- Previous PSA values and PSA trend
- Family history of prostate cancer
- Previous prostate biopsy
- Prostate volume
- PSA density
- Urinary tract infection or prostatitis symptoms
- Medication history
- Current medications
- Constitutional symptoms
- Bone pain or other symptoms suggestive of metastatic disease
- MRI findings
- Histopathological findings

## Evaluation Criteria

The AI-generated response should be evaluated according to:

- Clinical coherence
- Completeness of the diagnostic pathway
- Accuracy of recommendations
- Quality and relevance of sources
- Verifiability of references
- Appropriate level of clinical certainty
- Identification of missing information
- Clear distinction between evidence and interpretation

## Status

This is an educational proof-of-concept case.

It is not a validated clinical decision-support system and must not be used to make autonomous clinical decisions.
